1. Cyber-Physical Environment

The Core Principle: Manufacturing is Cyber-Physical
Manufacturing is a cyber-physical business where technology decisions affect production lines, quality, output, and delivery. Outages can stop lines, delay shipments, create scrap, or introduce safety risk.
Speaking Line: "Manufacturing is consequence-sensitive. A compute decision can affect not only users and data, but production continuity, quality, and recovery capability."

2. IT vs Operational Technology (OT)

Enterprise IT
  • ERP, Finance, Procurement
  • Corporate analytics
  • Standard change windows
  • Cloud-friendly architectures
Operational Technology (OT)
  • PLC, SCADA, DCS, HMI
  • Machine controllers
  • Production-constrained changes
  • Latency-sensitive, edge-connected

3. ISA-95 Integration Standard

ISA-95 defines boundaries between business systems and manufacturing control systems.

Level 4 (Enterprise): ERP, finance, procurement
Level 3 (Operations): MES, maintenance, quality
Level 2 (Control): SCADA, supervisory monitoring
Level 1/0 (Equipment): PLCs, sensors, actuators

4. The Downtime Imperative

!
Unplanned Downtime is Expensive
  • Lost output and missed deliveries
  • Overtime and rush logistics costs
  • Quality fallout and scrap
  • Customer and supplier escalations

Executive Strategy & Anti-Patterns

How to structure answers, avoid red flags, and demonstrate executive presence.

The Strong Answer Formula

1. State the Principle
"Enterprise standardization should focus on the control plane."
2. Define the Boundary
"Central owns identity, policy, landing zones, cost controls."
3. Explain the Trade-off
"Too much centralization slows adoption; too little creates fragmentation."
4. Give the Decision
"So I centralize baseline controls and federate workload design."
5. Show Operating Consequence
"That reduces exception debt and keeps onboarding fast."

Red Flag Answers to Avoid

❌ "Everything should eventually move to Azure."
Signals cloud ideology over operational fit.
❌ "OT should just follow the same standards as IT."
Shows ignorance of manufacturing reality.
❌ "My focus would be cost optimization first."
Ignores resilience and operational risk.
❌ "Compute is mainly about virtual machines."
Misses platform, container, edge, resilience.
Executive Presence Checklist

✓ Do This

  • Define boundaries clearly
  • Separate enterprise policy from workload design
  • Use repeatable decision frameworks
  • Show comfort saying "not as a default"
  • Speak in trade-offs, not absolutes
  • Treat governance as enabler

✗ Avoid This

  • Over-indexing on technical detail
  • Recommending services as personal preferences
  • Confusing architect and product owner roles
  • Treating governance as ticket review
  • Generic "best practice" answers
  • Avoiding hard decisions

First 90 Days — MAP Framework

Detailed execution plan: Map the estate, Architect the baseline, Pilot standardization.

Days 1-30: Establish Truth
Map the Estate
Inventory & Assessment:
  • Current Azure subscriptions and patterns
  • Workload inventory by business unit
  • Plant-adjacent dependencies
  • Identify unmanaged exceptions
  • Review top cost drivers
  • Security and resilience gaps
Stakeholder Alignment:
  • Meet with Plant IT leaders
  • Align with OT/security teams
  • Understand manufacturing SME concerns
Days 31-60: Define Product
Architect the Baseline
Product Definition:
  • Create compute product taxonomy
  • Define standard workload archetypes (7 patterns)
  • Define placement principles
  • Establish minimum security controls
Governance Framework:
  • Management group hierarchy
  • Policy inheritance model
  • Subscription vending process
  • Exception handling framework
Days 61-90: Execute
Pilot Standardization
Controlled Launch:
  • Launch first catalog-backed patterns
  • Start with high-confidence use cases
  • Rationalize top exception categories
  • Implement cost transparency
  • Build trust with early adopters
