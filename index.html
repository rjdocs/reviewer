Speaking lines

First 10 minutes of the interview

Use these lines to frame yourself as a revenue-minded technical partner. The tone should be calm, commercial, executive-friendly, and anchored on repeatable EMS solutioning—not raw protocol depth.

Structured opener
Sales-partner posture
0–2 min

I position myself as a technical-commercial partner to sales rather than a pure architect. My focus is to help teams win the right deals by shaping opportunities early, aligning them to repeatable managed service patterns, and ensuring what we sell can be delivered and supported cleanly.

2–5 min

In practice, I contribute in three areas. First, I improve qualification—understanding whether an opportunity is real, fundable, and aligned to the operating model. Second, I shape solutions that are technically credible but also commercially viable and repeatable. Third, I increase customer confidence by translating complex architectures into clear business outcomes, which helps accelerate decision-making.

5–7 min

For a managed services environment like EMS, I am very conscious that not every technically possible solution is a good solution. I anchor designs to standard patterns, lifecycle operability, and support boundaries so we avoid creating delivery risk or one-off architectures.

7–9 min

I work closely with account teams to position value, handle objections, and maintain deal momentum. My goal is to make the salesperson more effective—clearer messaging, stronger technical credibility, and fewer surprises late in the cycle.

9–10 min

Overall, I focus on improving win probability while protecting long-term customer success and operational integrity. That balance is what makes a strong partner to a Head of Sales.

Delivery notes

Keep your pace controlled. Do not sound rehearsed to the point of stiffness. Use short pauses after each block. Emphasize qualification, repeatable architecture, customer confidence, and delivery-risk control.

Do not start with products
Lead with outcomes
Tie to win rate
Protect supportability
  • Best posture: “I help sales win the right deals faster, with solutions that customers trust and delivery can execute.”
  • Best emphasis: show judgment on what to pursue, what to standardize, and where to phase.
  • Avoid: opening with a technology stack dump or speaking as if you are only a post-sales architect.
Whiteboard flow

15-minute whiteboard sequence

This is a Head-of-Sales whiteboard. Keep it commercially structured. The objective is to show that you can move from ambiguity to a winnable, supportable, and repeatable EMS solution.

Use black, green, red
Keep diagrams simple
0–2 min
Context framing
  • Draw: Customer → Problem → Outcome
  • Say: Start with business problem, constraints, and desired outcome.
  • Use green tags: speed, resilience, control, compliance, cost clarity.
2–5 min
Problem decomposition
  • Draw four branches: Application, Infrastructure, Risk, Operations.
  • Explain that this avoids jumping into premature solutioning.
  • Use red for downtime, compliance gaps, DR weakness.
5–9 min
EMS solution mapping
  • Draw managed private cloud core with vSphere, NSX, vSAN.
  • Add hybrid extension to AWS/Azure/GCP.
  • Add DR/Backup/Replication side branch.
  • Line to use: anchor to repeatable EMS architecture.
9–12 min
Value articulation
  • Draw Before → After: Complex to Simplified, Fragmented to Integrated, Reactive to Controlled, Uncertain to Predictable.
  • Position EMS as an operating-model improvement.
  • Use three pillars: operational simplicity, risk reduction, hybrid control.
12–14 min
Risk and differentiation
  • Draw risks: bespoke design, scope creep, misaligned expectations.
  • Contrast custom everything versus repeatable pattern.
  • Line: translate requirements into supportable patterns.
14–15 min
Close
  • Draw flow: Discover → Shape → Position → De-risk → Close → Handoff.
  • State that the role is to move from ambiguity to a winnable, supportable solution.
  • Final line: win the right deals, not just solve technical problems.

Whiteboard memory framework

P-S-V-R-C keeps the flow tight and executive-safe.

  • Problem: what is the customer actually trying to achieve?
  • Structure: break the issue into app, infrastructure, risk, and operations.
  • Value: translate EMS into operating-model improvement.
  • Risk: flag bespoke design, scope creep, and expectation mismatch.
  • Close: show the path from discovery to handoff.

Capstone scenario

Bespoke regulated hybrid-cloud request

A concise but difficult customer situation to prove that you can handle bespoke demands without collapsing into unsupported solutioning.

Bespoke request
Structured resolution

Problem

A large regulated customer wants a highly bespoke hybrid-cloud architecture with custom networking, non-standard disaster recovery, and exceptions to the managed-service model.

Risks

  • High revenue temptation
  • Low repeatability
  • High delivery risk
  • Unclear ownership boundaries
  • Potential break from EMS operating model

Approach

  • Separate true requirements from preferences by asking why each bespoke element exists.
  • Re-anchor the conversation on outcomes: resilience, compliance, performance, and operating control.
  • Map those outcomes to the closest standard EMS patterns first.
  • Use phased commitments: supported core architecture now, validated exceptions later if truly needed.
  • Be explicit about what is supported, what requires validation, and what sits outside the managed boundary.

Result

Customer feels heard, the design becomes largely standardized with controlled adaptation, deal momentum is preserved, and delivery risk is reduced.

I do not reject bespoke requests—I translate them into controlled, supportable outcomes.
Final reviewer notes

What the Head of Sales is deciding

The interviewer is not only assessing whether you are technically strong. He is deciding whether you are a reliable technical-commercial partner who will increase win quality, reduce presales drag, protect delivery, and create confidence in front of customers and internal teams.

Commercial judgment
Executive credibility

What to emphasize

  • Qualification discipline and selective pursuit
  • Repeatable EMS architectures over one-off customization
  • Translation of architecture into operating-model value
  • Executive-friendly communication with controlled depth

What to avoid

  • Opening with product catalog or protocol trivia
  • Sounding like a post-sales-only architect
  • Overpromising or vague enthusiasm without structure
  • Accepting bespoke asks without support-boundary control

Best anchor line

“I help sales win the right deals faster, with solutions that customers trust and delivery can execute.”
